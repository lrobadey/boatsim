# BoatSim — Story

Working story document. This records the current creative direction and separates it from proposed details. It does not authorize gameplay implementation.

## Direction established by the user

The game tells an authored story about a man forced from his home, beginning with his family and a small merchant ship. The player inhabits that story through sailing, trading, building, relationships, and eventually illegal activity.

The narrative should have a coherent protagonist and dramatic progression. Infinitely generated quests and extensive branching are not the goal. The protagonist can make authored decisions; player freedom can live in how situations are approached and experienced.

The Roman setting is preferred. Messana and the struggle surrounding Sextus Pompey's Sicilian power base are the current focus, with factions especially important. The exact starting year remains open.

### The opening

The protagonist has a pleasant property and a family. A veteran arrives with a higher-ranking figure who supports the takeover. They announce that the property is being taken for a military beneficiary or settlement; the exact legal mechanism and beneficiary remain to be chosen.

The newcomers may express some regret, but they intend to proceed. The protagonist is threatened and pushed off the land. He has no army or established resistance organization. He leaves with his family aboard his little merchant ship.

The first problem is finding somewhere to stay. The family sleeps aboard. Resentment and a desire for revenge develop after the loss.

The protagonist subsequently meets another displaced person who may become a companion and come aboard. Their identity, skills, and circumstances are not yet fixed.

### Home and revenge

The ship becomes the family's home. Construction and improvements should have practical, personal meaning: shelter, sleeping space, cooking, storage, privacy, and room for additional people.

The protagonist can become selfish. His desire for restitution or revenge need not remain morally admirable. A possible late confrontation involves an opponent offering to restore his house if he stops causing damage. The offer and the protagonist's response are not yet decided.

## Historical setting under consideration

Messana, modern Messina, sits on the Sicilian side of the strait separating Sicily from mainland Italy. It offers a compact connection between refuge, commercial movement, naval conflict, and hostile shores.

Two historical developments could ground the fictional personal story:

- After Philippi in 42 BCE, land confiscation and veteran settlement in Italy created dispossession and conflict. A flight associated with those events could precede the main game.
- Sextus Pompey's Sicilian position sheltered fugitives and supported maritime resistance. The Pact of Misenum in 39 BCE and the subsequent breakdown of peace offer a potential narrative frame. His defeat at Naulochus in 36 BCE provides a later historical horizon, not a committed ending.

The proscriptions beginning in 43 BCE and the later veteran land settlements are distinct processes. The protagonist is not automatically a formally proscribed man simply because he loses his property or flees.

Ancient accounts require critical treatment. Specific fictional people, motives, crimes, documents, and relationships should not be presented as documented history.

## Proposed protagonist — not yet locked

One candidate is a former municipal magistrate from a comfortable Italian family with commercial connections and access to a merchant vessel. This could explain his former standing, contacts, and expectations of fair treatment.

An earlier proposal made him an active opponent of confiscations who helped others escape. The user's later opening places the emphasis on his own dispossession and his family's departure. Prior resistance, diversion of public supplies, a violent confrontation, and formal criminal charges are therefore optional proposals, not established backstory.

His wife is **Terentia**. Her name and relationship to the protagonist are established decisions.

His original town, age, office, wealth, other family members, and political beliefs remain open. Terentia's background, personality, and role in the story remain to be developed.

### Name candidates

No name has been selected. The latest suggested working name was **Lucius**, with **Lucius Atilius Rufus** as a possible full name. Other suggested combinations were Marcus Vibius Varus, Gaius Terentius Rufus, Quintus Fabius Naso, and Titus Atilius Varus.

These are fictional candidates assembled from Roman name elements. Check the chosen combination against the eventual date, locality, and family background before treating it as final.

## Factions and relationships to develop

The following is a proposed dramatic structure, not an approved set of game systems or a claim that each group was a formal historical organization.

### The local antagonist

An invented local patron or official with connections to Octavian's settlement apparatus could benefit from the protagonist's removal. A former acquaintance or ally would make the conflict personal.

The veteran who receives the property need not be the principal villain. He may believe the land is a reward earned through service. The higher-ranking figure's identity and relationship to him remain open.

### Sextus Pompey's coalition

Sextus can offer refuge and opportunities while expecting useful service in return. His followers need not share one objective. Dispossessed families may seek restoration; commanders may seek influence; fugitives may seek safety; people escaping slavery may seek durable freedom.

These differences could become acute when leaders negotiate peace. The protagonist's relationships with particular people should make the consequences tangible.

### Octavian and his representatives

Octavian's camp can be an opposing power with interests in settlement, supply, and defeating Sextus. Agrippa is a major naval figure in the later campaign. Local officers and intermediaries can be invented so that the protagonist's everyday conflicts occur at a believable personal scale.

### Antony and other patrons

Antony's distinct interests and connections could provide alternative protection, introductions, or obligations. Lepidus is also relevant to the later Sicilian campaign. Neither requires constant personal appearances by the historical leader.

### Commanders and shifting allegiances

Menodorus provides a historical example of a commander changing sides: his transfer of Sardinia to Octavian in 38 BCE could make established contacts and safe places suddenly dangerous. Any personal relationship between him and the protagonist would be invented.

### The people aboard and ashore

Family, the displaced companion, crew, provisioners, artisans, traders, and local households give the story its immediate stakes. They should have particular needs and disagreements rather than exist only to endorse the protagonist's cause.

## Dramatic possibilities proposed in discussion

- Establish ordinary life at the property before the takeover. Familiar actions and objects make the loss concrete.
- Have the newcomers start unloading while the protagonist is still arguing.
- Make the first night aboard uncomfortable and provisional. Improvements to the ship answer needs the player has experienced.
- Introduce the companion through mutual need, such as repair work in exchange for shelter or passage. A shipwright is one possible identity.
- Let the first act of revenge concern a specific possession or shipment connected to the dispossession. Success creates an incentive to repeat it.
- Use lawful trade to support the household and provide access, information, and relationships that also enable covert activity.
- Explore a widening gap between the protagonist's stated purpose and the costs his actions impose on those close to him.
- Consider the thematic arc: he builds a new home aboard while risking it in pursuit of the old one. This is a proposed interpretation, not a fixed ending.

## Decisions still needed

- Starting date, duration, and relationship to Misenum and the Sicilian war.
- Protagonist's name, other family members, former position, and initial character; Terentia's background and character.

- Companion's identity and relationship to the family.

- The authored dramatic turning points, climax, and ending.
- Whether major historical outcomes remain fixed or the story allows alternate history.
