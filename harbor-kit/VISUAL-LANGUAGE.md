# Ormos visual language

User direction, 13 October 2026: retain the appealing minimalism; close views should feel clear and deliberate, with good textures and less visual mess.

**Warm, restrained Mediterranean craft.** Broad limewashed forms, cut limestone, warm muted clay, weathered timber and quiet green accents. Construction gives each object its identity. Surface texture gives it tactility.

- Silhouette first, construction second, surface grain third. Fine detail should not break the broad reading of a building, vessel or fixture.
- Use consistent bevels and joint widths. Keep maintained masonry legible; soften edge irregularity and remove scattered decorative damage.
- Let light describe depth through real recesses, overlaps and thickness. Do not imitate depth with high-contrast mottling or doubled patterning.
- Keep color variation within a narrow family. Clay roof tiles should read as one roof, not a patchwork. Wood grain should follow the timber without becoming a striped graphic.
- Preserve a few purposeful details: rope bindings, ceramic handles, timber pegs, shutter divisions, roof overlaps. Avoid adding detail simply because it can be modeled.
- Ground plants and rocks in coherent masses. Foliage should read as an airy crown first, individual leaves second.
- Judge at both approach distance and 1–3 m. Check grazing light, shade and stable depth ordering; z-fighting and inconsistent normals are defects, not texture.

This is a stylistic environment, not a claim of archaeological reconstruction. It aims for clarity and material quality rather than photorealistic dirt or uniformly damaged assets.
